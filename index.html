Moderated Caucus 1 — Freedom of Navigation & Legal Framework

Suggested motion to raise

"Japan moves for a moderated caucus of 8 minutes, with 60 seconds per speaker, on the topic: Legal frameworks for protecting freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz."

Your 60-second speech
"Honorable Chair,

Under Article 38 of UNCLOS, all ships — commercial, civilian, military — enjoy the right of transit passage through international straits. This is not a privilege. It is international law.

Iran's repeated threats to close the Strait, and the pattern of tanker seizures by the IRGC — most recently in 2023 — constitute direct violations of these legal norms. Japan, as the single most economically dependent nation on this waterway, urges this Council to reaffirm UNCLOS as the binding framework.

We propose the Council pass a resolution explicitly invoking Chapter VI of the UN Charter, calling on all parties to respect transit passage rights, and establishing a UNSC-mandated maritime observation mission — independent of any single nation's command — to monitor compliance in real time.

Japan is not seeking confrontation with any state. Japan is seeking the rule of law. Because when the law fails in the Strait of Hormuz, every nation in this room pays the price at the pump, at the factory, and at the dinner table.

Thank you."
Key arguments to build on in debate
  • UNCLOS has 168 state parties — it is arguably the most universally ratified treaty in international law. Iran IS a signatory.
  • Transit passage rights (UNCLOS Art. 38–39) cannot be suspended even in wartime under customary international law.
  • UNSC Resolution 598 (Iran-Iraq war) already set precedent for UNSC intervention in Gulf maritime security.
  • Japan's independent MSDF deployment demonstrates that non-confrontational maritime presence is possible and legal.
  • A UNSC monitoring body is different from a military coalition — it has legitimacy, neutrality, and legal authority.
Counter arguments & rebuttals
  • If Iran/Russia says "Western interference": "Japan's proposal is through the UNSC — the most legitimate multilateral body. Japan itself chose NOT to join the US-led Operation Sentinel. This is precisely to ensure neutrality."
  • If someone says UNCLOS doesn't apply to military vessels: "Articles 38-40 apply to ALL ships including warships in international straits — this is settled law."
  • If USA pushes for military enforcement: "Japan urges restraint. Unilateral military action risks the very destabilization we seek to prevent."

Moderated Caucus 2 — Iran, Sanctions & Diplomatic De-escalation

Sanctions without diplomacy are a weapon without a target. Japan has seen this repeatedly in the Persian Gulf context.

Its collapse in 2018 — not through Iranian violation, but through unilateral US withdrawal — directly contributed to the escalation we now debate. Japan never abandoned its diplomatic relationship with Tehran precisely because we understood that coercion alone would not work.

Japan proposes that this Council formally endorse the resumption of JCPOA negotiations, tie any further sanctions to specific, verifiable Iranian actions — not geopolitical pressure — and establish a UN Special Envoy for Persian Gulf Security Dialogue, modeled on successful precedents in the Korean Peninsula and Afghanistan.

Japan has served as an intermediary between Iran and the international community. We are willing to host or co-host such dialogue. We call on Iran to return to the table, and we call on all P5 members to prioritize a negotiated solution over military posturing.

The Strait of Hormuz will only be truly secure when Iran feels secure. That requires engagement — not encirclement.

Thank you, Chair."

  • Iran's motivation to threaten Hormuz is directly tied to sanctions pressure and security fears — address the cause, not just the symptom.

How to move for unmod

"Japan moves for an unmoderated caucus of 15 minutes for the purpose of working paper drafting and bloc formation."

Move unmod when: you have a draft ready to show; you need to flip a swing vote; you want to merge two competing papers; or when debate is going in circles and you need to reset.

Offer Iran a face-saving clause: monitoring is of ALL vessels, not just Iranian military — parity framing.

📄 Working paper structure (propose this)
  • Section A: Reaffirmation of UNCLOS (easy consensus, everyone signs)
  • Section B: Diplomatic pathway — JCPOA, UN Special Envoy (China/Russia win here)
  • Section C: Maritime Monitoring Mission (your flagship — link to Section A for legal grounding)
  • Section D: Economic Stabilization Protocol — IEA coordination (keep it soft, non-binding to get more signatories)
⚡ Power move: Merge before voting

If another bloc has a competing paper, propose a merger. In UNSC, you only need 9 votes and no P5 veto. A merged paper with US + China + Japan as co-sponsors is almost unvetable. Sacrifice minor clauses to get the big ones through.
